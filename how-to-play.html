STEP 1

The goal of the game

Chess is played by two players, White and Black. White always moves first, and then you take turns, one move each.

You win by checkmating the other king. That means the king is being attacked and has no way to escape. You never actually capture the king. The game ends the moment it's trapped.

STEP 2

Setting up the board

  • The board has 64 squares in 8 rows and 8 columns.
  • Put the board so the bottom-right square is light. Remember: "light on the right."
  • Pawns fill the second row in front of everything else.
  • The back row goes: rook, knight, bishop, queen, king, bishop, knight, rook.
  • The queen goes on her own color. The white queen starts on a light square, and the black queen on a dark one.

STEP 3

How each piece moves

Pieces can't jump over other pieces (except the knight). To capture, move onto a square with an enemy piece and take it off the board. The numbers show roughly how much each piece is worth.

King

∞ priceless

Moves one square in any direction. The king is the most important piece. If it gets checkmated, the game is over. It can never move onto a square where it would be attacked.

Queen

9 points

Moves any number of squares in a straight line or diagonal. She combines the rook and the bishop, which makes her the strongest piece on the board.

Rook

5 points

Moves any number of squares up, down, left or right. Rooks are strongest on open columns and on the second-to-last row.

Bishop

3 points

Moves any number of squares diagonally. A bishop stays on the same color square for the whole game, so each side has a light-square bishop and a dark-square bishop.

Knight

3 points

Moves in an L shape: two squares one way, then one square to the side. It's the only piece that can jump over other pieces, which makes it great at surprise attacks called forks.

Pawn

1 point

Moves one square straight forward, never backward. On its very first move it may go two squares. Pawns are different from every other piece: they capture diagonally forward, one square.

Try it: on the knight's board, the knight is surrounded by its own pawns, but it can still jump out. On the pawn's board, push the pawn up and look for the diagonal capture.
STEP 4

Three special moves

Castling

Castling moves your king two squares toward a rook, and then the rook hops over to the other side of the king. It's the only time you move two pieces at once. It tucks your king somewhere safe.

  • Neither the king nor that rook can have moved yet.
  • No pieces can be between them.
  • You can't castle out of check, through check, or into check.

Promotion

When a pawn reaches the last row, it turns into any piece you want except a king. Almost everyone picks a queen. You can even have two or three queens!

En passant

A tricky pawn capture. If an enemy pawn moves two squares and lands right beside your pawn, you can capture it as if it had only moved one square. You must do it right away, on your very next move, or the chance is gone.

STEP 5

Check and checkmate

Check means the king is under attack. You must get out of check right away. There are three ways:

  • Move the king to a safe square.
  • Block the attack by putting a piece in the way.
  • Capture the piece that's giving check.

If none of those work, it's checkmate and the game is over.

Check: the rook attacks the king, but the king can step aside

In this position the rook gives check on the back row. The king's own pawns block every escape square, nothing can block, and nothing can capture the rook. That's checkmate. It's called a back rank mate, and it's one of the puzzles in ChessMD.

Mind tip: before every move, ask yourself, "What is my opponent threatening?" That one question prevents most checkmates.
Checkmate: Rd8#
STEP 6

When the game is a draw

Not every game has a winner. A draw counts as half a point for each player. The game is a draw when:

  • Stalemate: it's your turn, you're not in check, but you have no legal move.
  • Not enough pieces are left to checkmate, like king against king.
  • The same position repeats three times.
  • 50 moves pass with no capture and no pawn move.
  • Both players agree to a draw.
Watch out: when you're winning, make sure the other king always has a move. Stalemating by accident turns a win into a draw.

STEP 7

Reading chess moves

Each square has a name. The columns are letters a to h, and the rows are numbers 1 to 8, starting from White's side. So the bottom-left square is a1. A move is written as the piece's letter plus the square it goes to.

STEP 8

Beginner tips

  1. Control the centerStart with a center pawn like e4 or d4. Pieces in the middle reach more squares.
  2. Bring out your piecesMove knights and bishops out early instead of moving the same piece twice.
  3. Castle earlyGet your king to safety in the first 10 moves or so.
  4. Don't rush the queenIf she comes out too early, she gets chased around and wastes time.
  5. Checks, captures, threatsEvery move, look for these three things, for you and for your opponent.
  6. Breathe after a mistakeEveryone blunders. Take a slow breath, then look for your best move in the new position.
